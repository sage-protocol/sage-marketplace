# Research Basis — Label Every Required Point

## What skimming supports

Duggan and Payne (DOI 10.1037/a0016995), abstract-level. Readers given time for about half the text. Skimming beat reading a half for important ideas. It did not beat that half for unimportant details or for inferences across texts. It had no advantage over reading the first or second half of every paragraph. The important-idea advantage depended on page linkages. Gaze went earlier in the paragraph, to the top of the page, and to earlier pages. Readers satisfice.

Morkes and Nielsen, NN/g, 1 Jan 1997, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/. Against a promotional page: concise +58 percent, scannable +47 percent, objective +27 percent, combined +124 percent. The scannable version used bullets, bold keywords, captions, shorter sections, more headings, and some inverted pyramid. The axes were tested as dichotomies. This is a web usability report, not a journal article and not a chat experiment. The 6 Jan 1998 Sun rewrite cut word count to 54 percent. The articles page states 159 percent usability for that rewrite.

Hartley: structured abstracts may be easier to read, sometimes (1997, DOI 10.1111/1467-9817.00025). In 30 pairs they were more readable, longer, and more informative, and judges were faster with less difficulty (1998, DOI 10.1111/j.2044-8279.1998.tb01303.x). With content matched, layout plus subheadings drove higher ratings in two of three studies. Study 2, 225 academics, found no main effect (Hartley and Betts 2007, DOI 10.1002/asi.20718). These are abstracts, not chat.

Lorch and colleagues, from abstracts and search extracts, not full PDFs, and from expository text, not chat:

- 1993, Journal of Educational Psychology 85(2). Headings, overviews, and summaries improved memory for topics and organization. Signals produced more topics remembered and less about each topic.
- 1996, Journal of Educational Psychology 88(1). No effect when structure was simple. When structure was complex, signaling changed what was recalled and how it was organized. Half-signaled text: unsignaled content was recalled worse than in a fully unsignaled text.
- 1996, Contemporary Educational Psychology 21(3), DOI 10.1006/ceps.1996.0022. Headings helped free recall of unfamiliar topics, not familiar ones. On summaries, headings raised inclusion of briefly discussed topics.
- 2001, Contemporary Educational Psychology 26, summarization. Signaled topics were more likely to appear in a summary. Half-signaling magnified that. Signaled topics went up. Unsignaled topics went down.
- Sanchez, Lorch, and Lorch 2001, DOI 10.1006/ceps.2000.1056. Outline training, headings, or both improved memory for topics and organization.
- Lorch, Lemarié, and Grant 2011, DOI 10.1080/10888431003747535. Hierarchical headings produced better outlines. Sequential headings sped search more than hierarchical headings.
- Lorch, Lemarié, and Chen 2013. Subtopic memory was better with headings than with preview sentences that carried the same topic information. No difference on simple facts. Outlining was better with either signal than with none.

The half-signaling result is the operative one: a partial list is worse than no list for the points you left out.

## Why one message, not a thread per point

Hewitt 2003 (DOI 10.2190/pmg8-a05j-cuh1-dk14): a recency bias elongates notes. Hewitt 2005 (DOI 10.1207/s15327809jls1404_4): focus on unread notes can starve threads. Suthers et al. 2008 (DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2006.10.007): threads were associated with coherence and convergence problems. Herring 1999: a subject line often fails as the topical unit, and loosened coherence is sometimes a use, not a defect. Do not answer a multi-point ask by opening one thread per point in a busy channel.

Kummerfeld et al. 2019 (DOI 10.18653/v1/P19-1374) is a corpus result: in one widely used dialogue corpus, 80 percent of conversations were missing messages or contained extra messages. Use it as a reason the pointer has to survive a messy log.

## Scope & source limits

Full Lorch PDFs were not opened. Duggan and Payne were used at abstract level. The Morkes and Nielsen combined score is against a promotional web page. Hartley's structure effect was absent as a main effect in one of three studies, and the structured abstracts were often longer.
