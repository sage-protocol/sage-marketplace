# Research Basis — Address the Prior Turn

## Why the pointer exists

Standard chat breaks the link between a turn and its reply. Smith, Cadiz, and Burkhalter (MSR-TR-2000-43, conference DOI 10.1145/358916.358980) note that this rupture is where standard chat introduces ambiguity most profoundly. Their threaded design was equally effective, different, and possibly more efficient, and it added interface challenges of its own. Keep "possibly." Do not read that paper as "deeper trees are always better."

Herring (HICSS 1999, DOI 10.1109/hicss.1999.772674, journal DOI 10.1111/j.1083-6101.1999.tb00106.x, PDF pages 1 to 10) shows receipt order breaking adjacency. Repairs she records include addressivity, a linking phrase, a selected quote, and a system pointer. Nested quotes can become a joke about the quotes. Subject-line threads are often not the topical unit. Scrollback offsets some incoherence. Loosened coherence is sometimes what people want. Do not collapse the paper into "just thread it." The one-message rule for a busy channel lives in `label-every-required-point`.

Severinson Eklundh (Language@Internet 7, 2010, urn:nbn:de:0009-7-26651) found contextual quotation in 84 percent of 117 newsgroup Re: replies. Of 98 quoted replies, 59 quoted a selected part, 19 quoted the whole message, and 19 had a missing antecedent. About 73 percent placed the quote above the response. Private email in the same study quoted the whole message far more often. A 1994 survey, as that 2010 article reports it and not from the 1994 paper itself: quoting was treated as essential on busy networks, almost 90 percent seldom or never quoted the whole text, and only 2 percent always placed the quote below the new text. The same article reports Herring 1994 treating a quote of the entire previous message as one of the most negative net behaviors. Clients that insert the whole message by default produce whole-message quotes.

Brennan and Clark (DOI 10.1037/0278-7393.22.6.1482) treat a shared name as a conceptual pact. The pact is historical and partner-specific. Speakers keep it even when a simpler reference exists, then simplify, and abandon it when the partner can no longer use it.
