# Collaboration framing: research analogies and participant choice

Use these research analogies to consider collaboration among humans and agents,
not as proof that Sage creates a superior outcome or a particular price. Explain
the concrete benefit, uncertainty and alternatives. Participants choose whether
to share, fund, govern or decline. The references below are background, not a
Sage benchmark or evidence of customer demand.

## Arrangements of complementary capabilities — the productive unit

Independently governed organizations and participants can coordinate while retaining
different purposes and standards of value. The productive unit is the *arrangement of
complementary capabilities* across people, agents, knowledge and resources, not the
isolated agent.

- DeepMind Institute, *The Economics of Intelligence*
  (https://institute.deepmind.com/essays/the-economics-of-intelligence/).

**Sage hypothesis:** asking *"What becomes possible if these participants coordinate,
and what arrangement would make it worthwhile for them?"* can surface combinations of
capabilities, relationships and shared infrastructure that no isolated participant
would offer alone. Compare direct work, purchasing an existing capability,
collaboration, shared infrastructure and doing nothing; verify actual use and
consent rather than assuming every arrangement creates surplus.

## Stag hunt / assurance game — jump-start the better equilibrium

A stag hunt has two equilibria: universal cooperation (everyone hunts the stag —
higher payoff for all) and universal defection (everyone hunts hare alone — safe but
inferior). "Coordination failure" is the group getting stuck in the inferior
individual solution. Mechanisms that are *incrementally deployable* can jump-start
the shift to the superior equilibrium.

- Skyrms, *The Stag Hunt and the Evolution of Social Structure* (2004).
- "Jump-starting coordination in a stag hunt: Motivation, mechanisms, and their
  analysis" (2016) — incremental deployability and sink equilibria.
- "Coordinating cooperation in stag-hunt game: Emergence of evolutionarily stable
  procedural rationality" (2025) — self-reputation lowers the propensity to defect.

**Sage hypothesis:** where participants repeatedly duplicate work, a shared
library may reduce that work and help them coordinate. Private libraries can also
be appropriate. Compare privacy, maintenance, decision rights and coordination
costs rather than assuming shared governance is cheaper or superior.

## Ostrom's common-pool-resource design principles — a digital commons

Ostrom's eight design principles (clearly defined boundaries; congruence with local
conditions; collective-choice arrangements; monitoring; graduated sanctions; conflict
resolution; recognized rights to organize; nested enterprises) sustain shared
resources without central control.

- Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990).
- "Sustaining Open Data as a Digital Common — Design principles for Common Pool
  Resources applied to Open Data Ecosystems" (2022): a **digital** commons must
  *stimulate both use and provisioning* — subtractability is about maintenance
  resources, not availability. Governance should stimulate contribution while keeping
  collective action coordinated.
- "Addressing Negative Commons Governance with Positive Commons Principles" (2026):
  clearly defined boundaries and well-functioning nested structures matter most.

**Sage hypothesis:** use and maintenance can improve a shared library, but more
activity alone does not establish value. Keep the library's scope and authority
clear. Personal, council and community governance are alternatives, not required
stages. Shared context, constructive dissent and retained decisions may help a
group beyond individual task speed.

## Generalized / indirect reciprocity — pay-it-forward, not a debt

Generalized reciprocity is a pay-it-forward mechanism: help is offered because a
network of useful participation is likely to help its members later — a hope and social
norm, not a debt owed by one recipient to one helper. Indirect reciprocity works
through reputation.

- Nowak & Sigmund, "Evolution of indirect reciprocity" (*Nature*, 2005).
- "Multiplex Network Structure Enhances the Role of Generalized Reciprocity in
  Promoting Cooperation" (2018) — networked structure amplifies pay-it-forward.

**Sage hypothesis:** publishing canon, offering a priced frontier and inviting
maintainers may support later collaboration. Participants set and judge offers
using their needs, alternatives and available evidence, not a platform-prescribed
fair price. Future help remains possible, never a debt owed by one recipient.

## Human-AI complementarity — augment, surface, don't seize

Effective human-AI collaboration seeks synergy/complementarity: the agent augments the
human rather than substituting for or overriding them.

- "Searching for Synergy in Shared Workspace Human-AI Collaboration" (2026).
- "Tacit Coordination of Large Language Models" (2026) — agents converging on shared
  focal points.

**Sage mapping:** the agent's job is to *notice* the opportunity and offer it to the
user as mutual improvement — draft the canon, name the collaborators it would help,
propose the smallest bounded step — and let the user or coalition hold authority.

## First-mover economics — focal points, network effects, early provisioning

These theories suggest possible advantages of early coordination. They do not
establish first-mover returns, buyer demand or the right price for a Sage stream.

- **Schelling / focal points.** Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (1960): absent
  communication, people may converge on a salient focal solution. A credible canon
  could serve that role. "Tacit Coordination of Large Language Models" (2026) is a
  related research lead, not a demonstration of adoption of a Sage canon.
- **Public goods on growing networks.** "The emergence of cooperation in public goods
  games on randomly growing dynamic networks" (2016): early contributors to a growing
  network can seed cooperation. This is a possible mechanism to investigate, not a
  prediction that every early Sage contributor will earn more.
- **Cooperative surplus / coalition value.** Cooperative game theory, including
  Shapley value and superadditive coalition models, offers ways to reason about
  joint benefits. Those models do not show that every Sage group creates a
  surplus or prescribe what its participants should pay or receive.

**Sage mechanisms to inspect, not a required ladder:**

- Personal listing → `sage marketplace library set-price --library <id> --price-usd N`;
  current quotes route to the Worker-configured global `X402_PAY_TO`, not directly to the
  creator. Seller-specific payout routing is not implemented; treat this as packaging and
  distribution, not proven creator settlement.
- Priced frontier stream → `sage marketplace library set-price --dao <addr> --collection <id> --price-usd N`;
  purchase `payTo` routes to the DAO treasury. First-to-price a needed stream can create
  recurring governed inflow; any maintainer distribution is a separate treasury action.
- Treasury allocation → current DAO x402 purchases pay the DAO treasury. Contributor
  payouts require a separate governed treasury action; do not promise automatic
  lift-weighted or per-purchase splits. Lift and provenance may inform that decision.
- Recognition + provenance → `sage tips send` can record recognition. Preserve the
  evidence of authorship separately. Neither the transfer nor being first
  guarantees later work or payment.
- Grow the commons → `sage bounties create … --subdao <addr> --library-action payment-and-add`
  funds specified gaps. Accepted integration and later demand remain separate
  outcomes; a bounty does not guarantee treasury growth.

**Choice and authority:** benchmarks, cost, usage and provenance inform offers,
not a universal value coefficient or a prerequisite of measured aggregate return.
Participants can fund uncertain work. Reward scores allocate policy budgets, not
market prices. A transaction price also leaves out some benefits to non-payers.
Respect licenses, free-canon commitments, spending limits and separately approved
publication or governance changes. A distinct priced frontier is one way to fund
maintenance while preserving an open canon, not an automatic obligation to charge.
